A step-by-step account of how I onboard new staff and volunteers onto Microsoft 365 at Malcolms Anatomy Foundation (MAF), a fully remote international health nonprofit — based on onboarding requests I've handled directly.

## Situation

MAF operates as a distributed team across multiple countries, with no central office. When a new staff member or volunteer joins, they need a working Microsoft 365 account — email, Teams, and the right license — often on short notice, sometimes flagged as urgent by the team lead.

## Task

Set up a new user in the M365 tenant correctly the first time: right license, working login, clear onboarding communication — with no in-person handoff possible, since the whole team works remotely.

## Action

**1. Receive the request.** A team lead submits the request for a new account, sometimes marked urgent.

**2. Create the account.** In Microsoft 365 Admin Center, I create the new user with their name and organizational email address.

**3. Assign the license.** I assign the license tier appropriate to their role.

**4. Verify the details.** Before sending anything, I double-check the account — correct name, correct email format, correct license — since mistakes here are harder to untangle once someone starts using the account.

**5. Send the onboarding email.** I send login details and setup instructions via Outlook so the new team member can access their account independently, without needing a live walkthrough.

**6. Confirm with the team lead.** I inform the requesting supervisor once setup is complete, closing the loop so they know the person is ready to start.

I've run this process independently multiple times, including under urgent, same-day requests — which meant working carefully but quickly, making sure urgency didn't cause a skipped step.

## Result

Each account was successfully created, licensed, and handed off, with the new team member able to log in and the supervisor confirming the setup was complete.

## Reflection

Doing this repeatedly, under both routine and urgent conditions, showed me where the process could be tightened. My main takeaway: build a simple onboarding checklist. Even a short one would help keep every setup consistent, regardless of how much time pressure is attached to the request — reducing reliance on memory alone when things move fast.
